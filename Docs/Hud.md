# HUD (inspired by the source game's layout)

The layout follows a screenshot of the source game, but all the art is generated by us (none of its files).

| Where | What | Class |
|-------|------|-------|
| Top left | Player frame: a round portrait (the character's current frame), level badge, name and class, HP (red) and mana (blue) with % | `UnitFrame` (LocalPlayer) |
| Top center | Target frame, mirrored (portrait on the right), name red for enemies, blue for players | `UnitFrame` (Target) |
| Top right | Zone name (Greenvale / the dungeon's name) and the minimap: ground, obstacles, dots – you (white), players (blue), monsters (red), vendors (yellow), portals (purple) | `Minimap` |
| Bottom left | Message log: loot, gold, experience, levels, warnings (`MessageLog.Add`) | `MessageLog` |
| Bottom center | Action bar in a frame: 12 slots (keys 1–9, 0, -, =) with Attack, spells and potions (with the count left), arranged by drag and drop (see [Crafting.md](Crafting.md)); a thin experience bar under it | `AbilityBar`, `ExperienceBar` |
| Above monsters | Red names above the HP bar (the selected one with its level), other players' names blue; monster HP bars always visible | `UnitNameplates`, `HealthPresenter` |

The minimap picture is drawn once per map from `WorldNavigation` (walkable / blocked),
the zone name comes from `WorldNavigation.ZoneName`.

## Esc and popups

- Esc closes the open windows first (character C, bag I, abilities P, vendor, loot, transmute, waystones, map); only
  when no window is open (or closing) does it drop the target and cancel a cast (`UI/OpenWindow.cs`). With a gem being
  set into an item, Esc cancels that and keeps the bag open.
- Tooltips are drawn over every window (a canvas of their own sorted on top) and kept on the screen.
- Gear in the bag or the loot window is compared with what is worn (`ItemTooltip`, `Inventory.BonusOf`): the worn piece
  (both rings) shows beside the tooltip under "Equipped", and the tooltip lists "If worn instead:" what would change
  (weapon value, armor, health, mana, every attribute; green better, red worse).
- The HUD texts are rewritten only when what they show changes (the action bar, nameplates, frames, the bag, the
  character sheet, the vendor list a few times a second), to keep WebGL frames smooth.

## Performance overlay (F8)

F8 (not F3: browsers search the page with it) shows frames per second, the slowest frame of the last second (ms), the
spawned units and monsters and the managed memory, updated once a second and when it opens (`UI/PerformanceOverlay.cs`,
its own canvas, under the minimap). Use it to check a change against a target such as a steady 60 FPS: walk the same
route before and after.

## Death panel

When the local player dies, a panel says "You have died." (the source game's "You have died. Respawn?") with a Respawn
button (`UI/DeathPanel.cs`): after 1.5 sec it brings the player back at the spawn point with full health and mana
(`PlayerRespawn.WaitForChoice`). Until then a friend's Deliverance can revive them where they fell. Test bots press it
themselves.

## Cheats (F9)

For testing, `Cheats` (on the player) and `CheatWindow` (F9). Only the host's own character may use them (offline
single player, or the one hosting); the server checks it. Buttons: Level +1, Level 25, +5000 XP, +10000 gold, Heal + mana
(and harmful effects removed), Kill around (monsters within 8 units), God mode (no damage, health and mana kept full),
Fast (Haste +200 %), All spells (every spell of the class learned), Potions and food (10 of each), Gems (3 of each), Orbs,
keys and scrolls (1 of each), Gear of my level (one piece of the class's gear per slot), Empty the bag, All waypoints,
and a Teleport button for every waypoint.
